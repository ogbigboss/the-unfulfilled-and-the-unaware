### 11.6.2040

The newly elected President of the United States stepped up to the podium to deliver their acceptance speech. After the basic intro, followed by the perfunctory performative applause, the crowd simmered down and listened.

"Close to twenty years ago, this country could only agree to one thing - to disagree. Half the country felt we lacked a shared epistemology, while the other half felt we lived in a post fact world, which somewhat ironically meant the same thing. I'm proud to say this country has made great strides since those darker times."

More applause, then as silence returned, the former Senator and Congress person continued -

"Around this time, as many of you know, I was asked to research therapy, and with a group of my colleagues, formed connections between both parties that has lasted and thrived to this day. We discovered that certain therapies, under the scope of Aaron T. Beck's Cognitive Behavioral Therapy, wasn't just useful for treating severe mental illness, or mental illness at all. Since then we've transformed these learnings into Congitive Behavioral _Techniques_."

The crowd, composed of constituents from both parties, applauded in recognition of this reframing, one many had adapted to themselves, the rest saying they did just to seem as though they kept pace with the times.

"Around ten years ago, Congress pass the CBT Proliferation Act, providing funding and training to the leadership class in America on Cognitive Behavioral Techniques, to provide the benefit of Dr. Beck's research without the previous stigma of diagnosis. Since then, we're a calmer nation - accepting of divergent views without growing intolerant at disagreements. Truly, we as a country can proudly say we represent F. Scott Fitzgerald's opinion on what qualifies a first rate intelligence: the ability to hold two opposed ideas in mind at the same time, and still retain the ability to function. To my constituents - of both parties, of all parties - I am proud to say that this has become a country that can function, and will continue to."

Rabid, manic applause insues - the applause of a hope that could be believed by a greatness that had been provably achieved, and a confidence in change for the better as an inevitablity. The world had finally become flat, or flatter, for now.

Somewhere, The Man watched this on TV, grimacing to himself. He had, for nearly 40 years, stored tension on the right side of his body, which occasionally caused his head to lean to his right. And he thought to himself - wait, fuck, I'm not trying to disagree. Are they still watching me?

He didn't ruminate on it much farther - if they were, after all that therapy, maybe he had finally taught the leaders of the world to lighten up and take a joke - it was the easiest way to deal with something you couldn't confirm to be true.

Deny, deny, deny - but laugh a little on the sly. Just in case, the man tilted his head to his left, offered some applause, and turned off the glowing screen, as he was most fond of doing.
