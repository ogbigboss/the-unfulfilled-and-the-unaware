## 4.20.2025

Horatio, personal friend to Art and an M&A specialist from Horatio Holdings (with no relation to the founder, now deceased, Gilbert Horatio), spent the evening practicing, in preparation for the next day when he would try to explain things to Art, again.

Polonium, the Pequod News Network, and Control Room Karen's attempts to "influence" The Man via paid actors, retrofitting national news, and television advertisement manipulation was quite simply backfiring. Horatio Holdings' financial analysts, used to looking into situations of all types given the Holding company's diverse portfolio, had arrived to a conclusion, which The Man proved correct more often than not. He used the attempts to influence him to decipher what it was they actually wanted, what it was they were afraid of, and played that to his advantage, not to come out victorious, but to escape their clutches.

They were better off letting The Man go at this point, and that wasn't Art's fault - Horatio reminded himself to stress that point to Art - it was the people Art hired to gaslight - "influence", Horatio reminded himself to say - The Man. There were further conversations to have after that, all ones that Horatio Holding's financial analysts could take from there. All of that came later, the first step was just in convincing Art to let The Man go, and that would necessitate Art feeling as though the opportunity to gaslight The Man into more than a depressive state was not his fault.

Previously Horatio had tried explaining this as a matter of legal liabilities, to which Art kept laughing off, saying he owned the law, that he sleeps next to it every night. Horatio sighed at this, knowing Art's husband Roman wasn't as powerful as Art perceived him to be.

Speaking of which, if he could get Art to stop saying things like "don't worry, just a little more time and we'll get the fucker to kill himself" around witnesses, that would be good as well. One thing at a time, thought Horatio.

...

Polonium's producers shook with fear. Their industry was contracting, they weren't a perfect fit for their new financial owner's portfolio, and they were embarrassing themselves in front of them. They had one chance at survival, or at least, avoiding a lesser fate than their current status.

They needed to prove to Art that they were indeed gaslighting the Man effectively - he wouldn't of bought their company for any other reason, it was in fact the only reason he did. He could have, and probably should have, selected a number of other unscripted content companies, he only chose Polonium because their name used to be Mental Hospital - something they changed before joining, feeling it was not a good look considering Art's Excursion Group was buying them for the sole purpose of gaslighting a man into insanity.

They even got a stock bonus if they could get The Man to kill himself, which was of course agreed to with a handshake and a wink - no paper work.

...

The Man wondered what his life would be like without CBT. He might've killed himself a long time ago, probably. With all `the Inner Party` had done to him, he would've definitely killed himself over the last three years, without CBT.

He knew how to use the tools of CBT to get him away from such thoughts, or to not allow them to build. Otherwise he wouldn't have made the choices he did which entangled his destiny with `the Inner Party` - he knew they would never gaslight him to that point, or to the point of developing co-morbidities purposefully to prove a diagnosis they'd prefer. He didn't fear them.

But he was depressed. Perhaps now more than ever. `The Inner Party` had manufactured a situation where everyone he ever knew likely heard that The Man was on a Reality TV show meant to prove whether he was schizophrenic or borderline, leaving no room for the truth, which was neither. A thick portion of the entertainment industry, which the Man was not a part of, were under the same illusion. He couldn't even seek the refuge of entertainment, music, movies, television, video games, without having to consider if the person who made it knew about The Man in this way, or if they had made the art under that illusion and at the coordination of `The Inner Party`. Anyone he met in the future, if they didn't know about this, would be a risk to find out. As a result, the Man trusted no one, often imagining a future as a total recluse, and that depressed him greatly given how many knew of him.

That's leaving out the lie, as well. At least he could prove that incorrect, but did he have to carry around a car theft report wherever he went to do so? The Man didn't have any easy answers to this, and he knew `the Inner Party` was relying on that. He greatly feared `the Inner Party` working to generate another false claim in the future, and accepted he'd likely have to be alone for the rest of his life just to protect himself from them.

CBT techniques for reversing negative thought patterns typically involve a step where one reframes the negative as something else, something more positive. The repetition of the technique makes it so eventually, one almost "evolves", to the point where if the initial thought was triggered, the negative side wasn't pursued independently, and was always paired with the re-framing, eventually just leaving the latter.

_Do you want to change your mind? You can always change your mind._

The Man hoped CBT could help him in the future, but he wasn't there yet, so as for his trust issues, he used them to benefit his current situation - it was better to not trust anyone when `the Inner Party` was trying to gaslight you into a mental diagnosis, after all. When this was over, he knew he had to reestablish trust, and figure'd he start with a dog before getting around to humans. For now, the idea of trust, and how much `the Inner Party` had damaged his, was one he just tried to keep out of his mind, when he could. Seeing dogs depressed him, at least today.

That said, his usual strategy for dealing with this situation was to reframe it as positive, and in a grandiose manner - that he mattered in a way most individuals would never, that his very breath made a point of a staggering importance, one to which would only be curtailed by letting go of his ability to continue breathing. `The Inner Party`, no matter how far and wide it went, would only break you if you let them.

Lately that wasn't as convincing.

With his newfound thinking - that `the Inner Party` would never push so far, and to such a wide breadth, for his misdiagnosis unless it meant that it spared them from their proper diagnosis - he was left feeling like an Atlas, the gravity of the globe on his shoulders, both pulling him in from it's center and crushing him at the same time.

Was this is eternity, left combating `the Inner Party`'s attempts to get him to kill himself, until he passed away naturally or otherwise?

He succumbed to depression. The thoughts of a life alone, without the ability to trust anyone, got to him. But he would not sink. He would never sink in it.

He would find a way to reframe this, to, and it would likely be to reinstate to himself his own importance to the world, at the risk of developing what he was considering to be the base condition of his detractors, narcissism.

The Man would just have to find a way to remain Atlas without becoming Narcissus. He'd find a way.

Just not today. Today, he just tried not to imagine a future, while trying not to imagine the easiest way one could forgo such a common fate. It wasn't easy.
