### What can we do about social media then?

When Facebook IPO'd, or was introduced to the public stock markets, it was unclear how to price their stock given their lack at the time of a revenue model. In lue of this, Facebook's user count, sign ups, "likes", "pokes", and other such interactions were used as metrics to justify it's stock price. Eventually Facebook, now Meta, made revenue such that it's stock price was held against dollars earned like most other companies, but the past remains something of a standard for many social media companies in making financial valuations.

The problem of malicious posts and posters is not one of technology or society or free speech - it's one of financial speculation. It's not hard to hire staff to identify malicious content and ban them at the root, nor is it impossible to ban users from returning once discovered. Social media companies and share holder boards are reluctant to be more forthright in banning or sidelining posts like this because the posts and the accounts behind such actions, while they do not result directly in revenue via advertising, still increase interaction counts, and thus are still used to justify stock pricing, due to the history of social media being nascent to markets in previous decades.

Financial speculators should adjust valuations to focus on revenue, not volume of interactions, for social media companies. We only did the latter because we weren't sure how to immediately price things, now we do. If valuations are measured against interactions (posts, sign ups, likes, etc) and not revenue, then there isn't a financial incentive to better police / ban / etc bad social media behavior, in fact it's the opposite.
