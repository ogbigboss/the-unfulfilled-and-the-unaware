### 11.6.2044

> "We are divided it seems; this is the diary of life in between."
>
> ― The Bronx, _Knifeman_

The newly elected President of the United States stepped up to the podium to deliver their acceptance speech. After the basic intro, followed by the perfunctory performative applause, the crowd simmered down and listened.

"Close to twenty years ago, this country could only agree to one thing - to disagree. Half the country felt we lacked a shared epistemology, while the other half felt we lived in a post fact world, which somewhat ironically meant the same thing. I'm proud to say this country has made great strides since those darker times."

The Man smiled as he thought to nearly twenty years ago, of being taken to that room which didn't technically exist, after the long car ride which technically didn't happen, and speaking to a nameless legal professional dressed in what was technically office wear.

...

"Look, what we're going to tell you is beyond classified - it's barely recorded in other contexts. If you attempt to share this information, it will be denied by any parties you could connect it to. Are we clear?" Between the two was a desk, with a manilla folder on one side, and a neatly stacked section of cinder blocks on the other.

"So this isn't actually happening?" said The Man, taking a slightly dour tone.

"And just so you know, there are no recording devices present, and furthermore, you're sitting inside a faraday cage, so no communications can come inside or out electronically. Check your phone to confirm, note the lack of bars on mine, and then just in case, put your phone with mine into this plastic box and we'll take them out of the room."

The Man confirmed his phone wasn't receiving any service. He had been patted down extremely thoroughly beforehand. He thought about asking if he could perform a cavity search on his new acquaintance - just in case - but thought the better of it. The Man put his phone in the plastic box, moved his ass funny in his chair to try and see if his new acquaintance would ask for a cavity search, and remained silent. The Man was looked at a bit funny, and his acquaintance seemed as though they were thinking the better of a fleeting thought better left unexplored.

"While there is the opportunity to pursue indictments for criminal medical malpractice against those that persued you, as well as other crimes, this will not come to pass. However, we can guarantee the criminal medical malpractice, the stalking, the slander, and, as you put it, the 'Orwellian situation' you're in, will come to a halt on the part of the political and private actors perpertrating this. Furthermore -"

The Man interrupted them - "well, the slander kind of makes it hard for me to go back to my normal career, doesn't it?" and was responded to with reasons why that shouldn't be a problem, reasons to which The Man found easy to accept.

"Furthermore, in exchange for their freedom from criminal prosecution, those that worked so hard to misdiagnose you have agreed to take part in a private, confidential, long term therapueatic program based on Cognitive Behavioral Therapy, such to prevent such tyranical behaviors occuring from the ownership of state actors ever again. This is at the direct advisement of Professor Noam Chomsky, whom `The Other Party` had requested provide his opinion on the situation. This program will continue and only end at the discretion of the FBI's BAU, Behavioral Analysis Unit, a part of the NC-"

"- the NCAVC, yeah - wait, for real? They're all going to talk therapy?"

"Well, no, not for real. None of this is actually happening. Take some time - all the details are in this manilla folder. When you're done, place the folder in these cinder blocks, and use the provided kerosene and matches to burn it all. Then leave the room, or you'll suffocate from carbon monoxide poisioning."

As his new acquaintance left, never to be seen again, the Man asked a question - "Hey, I didn't catch your name?"

"You -"

"I didn't?" the Man gave a coy smile out of the left side of his face, and his acquaintance left, grimacing. The Man thought he felt their eyes roll as he saw the back of their head.

For the first time in years, The Man was left alone in a room, left alone to read, and laugh, laugh, laugh his ass off. If he had stuffed a recording device inside of himself, he had also laughed so hard it would've surely come out of him with the laughter. He burned the folder after reading it and left.

...

More applause, then as silence returned, the President elect and former Senator and Congress person continued -

"Around this time close to two decades ago, as many of you know, I was asked to research therapy, and with a group of my colleagues, formed connections between both parties that has lasted and thrived to this day. We discovered that certain therapies, under the scope of Aaron T. Beck's Cognitive Behavioral Therapy, wasn't just useful for treating severe mental illness, or mental illness at all. Since then we've transformed these learnings into Congitive Behavioral _Techniques_."

The crowd, composed of constituents from both parties, applauded in recognition of this reframing, one many had adapted to themselves, the rest saying they did just to seem as though they kept pace with the times.

"Around ten years ago, Congress pass the CBT Proliferation Act, providing funding and training to the leadership class in America on Cognitive Behavioral Techniques, to provide the benefit of Dr. Beck's research without the previous stigma of diagnosis. Since then, we're a calmer nation - accepting of divergent views without growing intolerant at disagreements. Truly, we as a country can proudly say we represent F. Scott Fitzgerald's opinion on what qualifies a first rate intelligence: the ability to hold two opposed ideas in mind at the same time, and still retain the ability to function. To my constituents - of both parties, of all parties - I am proud to say that this has become a country that can function, and will continue to."

Rabid, manic applause insues - the applause of a hope that could be believed by a greatness that had been provably achieved, and a confidence in change for the better as an inevitablity. The world had finally become flat, or flatter, for now.

Somewhere, The Man watched this on TV, grimacing to himself. He had, for nearly 40 years, stored tension on the right side of his body, which occasionally caused his head to lean to his right. And he thought to himself - wait, fuck, I'm not trying to disagree. Are they still watching me?

He didn't ruminate on it much farther - if they were, after all that therapy, maybe he had finally taught the leaders of the world to lighten up and take a joke - it was the easiest way to deal with something you couldn't confirm to be true.

Deny, deny, deny - but laugh a little on the sly. Just in case, The Man tilted his head to his left, offered some applause, and moved to turn off the glowing screen, as he was most fond of doing.

Just before The Man could turn the screen off, he heard the announcer commenting on the color of the President elect's hair - "Just like Thomas Jefferson's, provided Jefferson took his wig off first." The thought echoed in The Man's head, leaving him alone with the unconscious weight of a world.
