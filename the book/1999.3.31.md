# Prologue

1600 - Shakespeare writes _Hamlet_.

1851 - Melville publishes _Moby Dick_.

1908 - G.K. Chesterton publishes _The Man Who Was Thusday_.

1949 - Orwell publishes _1984_.

1988 - Herman and Chomsky publish _Manufacturing Consent_.

## 3.31.1999

The Boy sat in a movie theater in Cedar Grove, New Jersey, named Cinema 23. He sat facing to the west to look at the screen. Further west was Pompton Avenue, past whatever is typically behind a movie's fourth wall in a theater. It was opening night for _The Mimus_, starring Will Smith alongside Gina Gershon.

The Boy sat between his two friends, all 8th graders, which required one of their parents to buy tickets for the Rated R movie as they were all 13. Said parent was to pick them up at the movie's end after their son called him collect on a soon-to-be ancient payphone in the lobby. The Boy hadn't wished to see _The Mimus_ - he was outvoted two to one by his friends, his loser of a vote going to _Office Space_.

The boys were transfixed to the screen nearly two hours into the perfectly edited movie - none of them had even thought to go to the bathroom despite their recently digested popcorn, jumbo-sized sodas, and large bags of candy. The film described a world that was secretly an illusion managed by highly developed computers that enslaved humanity and trapped them in a fake world called _The Mimus_ - the film's title. It was similar to _The Terminator_ series, but instead of time-traveling robots committed to end humanity, _The Mimus_'s robots froze time in the late 90s and trick humanity into living a false reality.

The movie kept perfect pacing to the typical hero's journey, with ground-breaking special effects that made it easy to think of nothing else while it was being played, especially for teenage boys. Near the end, the robots shoot Will Smith's character - the hero - in the digital world. In the "real world," Gina Gershon's character, TreLala, saves Will Smith's character's physical and digital forms - with a kiss.

The Boy rolled his eyes - he was lucky enough to have already been kissed, and had since decided it wasn't the type of thing to save a life with. He figured the writers ran out of ideas as to how to tie it all together, or needed to sell the script to studios that sought repetition in pursuit of repetitive sales. This was taught to him by his 6th grade teacher Mrs. Driscoll's bending of her English class to make certain Fridays about her passion, film. As a result, The Boy, while as easily entertained as the average 13 year old male, was also taught to be somewhat cynical of the things that entertained him. The writers needed special effects to sell their movie after all - they were probably hacks.

And if the writers of _The Mimus_ weren't hacks, then the Boy and his two friends probably wouldn't have been the only three people to be seeing the movie on opening night of all things.

After the kiss scene, Will Smith's character reanimates to another form in the digital world, one that was able to take the bullets that were shot into his stomach out of him, sparing him from death. His counterpart, an artificial intelligence named simply Agent Emanuel - played by fellow musician Phil Collins - stared agape at the scene, beginning to say "no - it can't be true, he can't be the -"

Will Smith's character cuts off Phil Collins' to say what would become the catchphrase of the movie, with Smith cocking his head sharply to his left as he said the word "right".

"It's damn right I'm The Uno!" The bullets that were once in The Uno's stomach floated above him and pointed towards Agent Emanuel. "Now draw four!" shouted The Uno, as the bullets changed to "draw four" cards from the card game Uno and ignited in flame, piercing Agent Emanuel with the force of the bullets that were once his.

Mattel would later sue _The Mimus_'s studio, only to later drop the suit in favor of a cross marketing promotion involving a redesign of the original Uno cards superimposed with the characters in the movie _The Mimus_.

## 07.29.2001

Some two and a half years later, mere months before the World Trade Center's twin towers fell victim to Osama Bin Laden's Al Qaeda led terrorist attack, The Boy was in summer camp, between his freshman and sophomore years in high school. It was a "pre college" camp, where parents paid money to send their children to a summer camp which was meant to simulate the college experience.

Many of his friends, it seemed like most of the camp, had opted into a class The Boy hadn't - an intro to philosophy. That evening, in the dormitory area of Drew University in Madison, NJ, the conversation was ablaze - the philosophy instructor, an undergraduate student whom was one of the camp counselors, had let the kids watch _The Mimus_. The Boy's camp mates were having a discussion on it's philosophical implications as The Boy picked at his guitar, an Ovation Pro Series, idly listening as he did so.

"So, like, the point is, what if our world wasn't real? Like, what if it was run by computers or something?"

"Well, that's not exactly the point - the computer thing is just the example in the movie."

The Boy, who was mindlessly playing blues riffs in A Minor, shifted to Metallica's _Master of Puppets_.

_Master of Puppets, I'm pulling your strings..._

"Yeah, I know, but how else could this world be fake? Wouldn't computers _have_ to be involved?"

"That's what the professor was stressing" - the term "professor" was used to heighten a sense of reality to the fake college experience - "the computer thing isn't the _philosophical_ part, it's the idea that our world can be a lie that we accept which is actually manufactured by others."

No one who knew the Metallica song was paying attention to The Boy, and others weren't as impressed with The Boy, whose rhythm while down picking was atrocious.

"Oh, please -" said someone outside of the philosophy class "- that sounds a bit dramatic. How could you know that to be true, that the world was a giant lie?"

"Well, if they were lying to you, you wouldn't know at all!"

The Boy shifted to the main riff in Stevie Wonder's _Superstitious_, which he admittedly learned via Stevie Ray Vaughan, a rite of passage for all guitar players after the cover had been recorded.

_Very Superstitious... wash your face and hands..._

"Well, that's not my point" said the person outside of the philosophy class. "Leaders lie to people all the time, think about the recent White House scandal. It took a lot to find out what was true after the President's initial denial. Or think about the recent election - how does a vote end up decided by the Supreme Court? Do we really know all the details there?"

"But that's _exactly_ the point - leaders lie and they get away with it so often that it's only every now and then we find out the truth. So who knows how much of our world, which they lead, is actually a lie! The computers aren't the point!"

A camp counselor sat closely by, idly listening, hoping to lead the conversation away from scandals they felt were inappropriate for the kids to discuss, or rather, they felt fear for being held responsible for allowing them to discuss, and decided to join in briefly to divert the conversation: 

"Well, look kiddos, what if _The Mimus_ was more than just a comment on leadership? What if we all participate unknowingly in accepting things which aren't fully true in ways that seem true?"

"Like what?" asked one of the campers, who regularly kissed the counselors asses out of habits well worn into them from parents, and school teachers, and other such authority figures.

"Well, would it be wrong if a boy liked another boy? Like, _liked_ him liked him. Or a girl another girl? And if you think it is wrong, what guides that opinion?"

The Boy, who was tired of being ignored, started to play the backing riff to TLC's _No Scrubs_.

_Can't get with a deadbeat ass, so, no, I don't want your number..._

"Oh. My. God. Is that TLC?" said one of the campers who was as much a non participant in the conversation as The Boy, looking at him in admiration. "I can't believe you know that one!"

The attention had shifted his way. He had done it on purpose, he was tired of hearing about _The Mimus_. Thinking back to two years ago, The Boy felt he was correct to have voted for _Office Space_, a movie he had seen a year ago on video cassette and arguably understood less than _The Mimus_. The Boy felt his fellow camper's conversation to be trivial in nature - wasn't it obvious, thought The Boy, that society forms conceptions which, over time, were later challenged to be seen as false? He didn't think _The Mimus_ was really that deep, and had a sneaking suspicion that _Office Space_ spoke more truth to the world, though he had yet to become a computer programmer such to find out either way.

"Ok ok ok - do you know any other songs we'd recognize?" a girl asked The Boy. The Boy knew of just the one, and started strumming something close to the opening chords of John Mayer's _No Such Thing_ - two chords which were a variation of one, E Major. The girl's eyes lit up, and others looked his way, recognizing the rhythm and the opening chord, an EMaj9. The Boy had to sheepishly admit that he only knew the opening two chords - the rest were too difficult for him to understand, all jazzy and what not. After a moment of disappointment, one of the girls realized something.

"Wait... Wait... Wait! What's the chorus to that song again?"

The Boy reminded the girl he didn't know the rest of the song.

"No, stupid, the lyrics!"

"Oh," offered someone else, "I know them - it's something like _I wanna run through the halls of my high school... I wanna scream at the top of my lungs..._ uhm... There's more..."

"Speaking of hotties, did you hear that James Van Der Beek graduated from this University?" another interrupted.

"Oh my god, I love that Paula Cole song! Do you know that one?"

"Uhm, excuse me? Can we finish the lyrics to the John Mayer song first, please? There's something here... I just can't remember the words..."

Someone else chimed in with more lyrics, providing the end of the chorus to Mayer's _No Such Thing_ -

_I just found out there's no such thing as the real world, just a lie you've got to rise above._

"Oh. My. Fucking. God! Is John Mayer talking about _The Mimus_?? Oh my god. He has to be! He has to!"

"That means John Mayer's the _real_ Uno!"

The entire scene broke into laughter at the idea of a white man, John Mayer, being the real version of a character played by a black man, Will Smith.

"Oh my god, can you even imagine his white ass saying 'Draw Four'? Or John Mayer being _The Fresh Prince of Bel-Air_?" More laughter at obvious absurdities, a laughter that goes on for a bit, ebbing and flowing until they all fell somewhat silent, waiting for the next card to be dealt.

"There's no way John Mayer could ever be The Uno if you ask me." This was flatly said by the camp's most outspoken fan of _The Mimus_, which caused the crowd to turn, one asking the obvious question of "Why not?"

"Well, leaving aside my crush on Jennifer Love Hewitt, to me, John Mayer can't be The Uno - he's just too much of a narcissist; he only cares about fame."
