## 5.29.2024

Dr. Antoinette Burjest stood alone in her kitchen peeling an orange, taking a second to check the time on her phone. As she did so, an alert popped up for a new email (though, as a woman in her eighties, she preferred the term electronic mail such to remind herself and anyone else involved that for the majority of her life, she had hand written most such things) from one of the lead producers of the potential _Thought Patrol_ spin off about her in the current day, a change from the original show which was rooted in her past. The email's subject was simply the word emergency, in all caps. Dr. Burjest preferred to check important emails on her desktop computer, and with a gravity that helped to work against the center of the planet, moved with haste to her home office.

She couldn't believe what she was reading. It was a short email, just a few sentences, but she re read it. Then, she read it again.

The Man was innocent of the worst accusation levied against him - there was a car theft that took place on the same day, with a story that negated the one told of him. She scanned the attachments to see a matching insurance report from over twenty years ago.

Dr. Burjest had been lied to. Yes, the accusation was a lie, and technically one told to her directly, but that wasn't the lie she was thinking of.

Jack Dugin had also lied to her.

Dugin knew more than he had told Dr. Burjest over a year ago. He had originally said he wanted to see The Man hung, tarred, and feathered - then things changed as 2022 rolled on.

First, he seemingly stepped away from the limelight after his _MasterTeach_ show came out, shortly before The Man was accused, focusing instead on his low rent Elton John cover band, Jack and the Bennies, which was struggling due to their low energy, too tall for his own good keyboard player, who suffered quietly from narcissistic personality disorder due to his insecurity of not being able to play basketball professionally despite his height. To Dr. Burjest, Dugin hid behind this story such to block the sunlight of the truth.

Later the next year, he had gotten _Thought Patrol_ cancelled without consulting her first - sure, it was based on his book, and he was the actual FBI agent while she was a nurse consulting with the FBI, but while it was his book, it was her _life_ that made the show special! She scorned the day he took that away from her. She asked him why, why in gods name would he do such a thing, and he didn't respond. When she pressed him, was it about The Man - he said yes, that he believed he was innocent, and couldn't say anything more as to why.

He then warned her that this was all political, and that he wouldn't risk his reputation on it. As he left, she asked - "But you were willing to risk my reputation, weren't you?" Jack simply turned around, grunted, and then turned his back to her, walking away.

It didn't matter that Jack Dugin had been right to do so - what mattered to Dr. Burjest in that moment was that he didn't tell her _why_ - he must have known about the car theft, probably through some contact of his who had a child that still worked in the Bureau, but he didn't tell her. Did he not trust her? They had spent nearly a decade working together in the 80s as part of the FBI's then Behavioral Science Unit, now the Behavioral Analysis Unit, and he knew she was well versed in "classified" details. What had happened since then that he lost trust in her?

Had he spent too much time in Hollywood, having inspired _The Silence of the Lambs_ and so much more, and did Jack not trust Dr. Burjest because she was only recently familiar with the lime light, her years of experience paling compared to his decades? Was he maybe jealous of her meteoric rise compared to his, which took more time?

It was a lot to process.

At the start of last year in 2023, _Thought Patrol_ was on the move to come back, but be placed in a modern context. As opposed to having a younger version of herself played by Anna Torv, Dr. Burjest would play her actual self. This worked as the first season would be all about The Man and how falsely he was portrayed in _The Mimus_. Dr. Burjest of the _Thought Patrol_ was the only person equipped to reveal the truth alongside the crime she was just learning he hadn't actually committed. It was perfect, as _The Mimus_ was about a thing which could only be real to a mentally broken mind - that the world was a lie! Also, since The Man was technically portrayed by Will Smith, there was a neat reflection with the fact that Dr. Burjest was played by Anna Torv, considering Torv's character's dual realities in the older fictional show _Fringe_. Not to mention that _Thought Patrol_ inspired the Wablonskys to cast Jonathan Groff as well as include an analyst character played by Neil Patrick Harris in the latest _Mimus_ film - it was fate that she, Dr. Burjest, was brought in to bring about some truth concerning The Man to counter the science fiction of _The Mimus_!

Jack Dugin, however, had voiced his displeasure of this concept at the end of 2022, stating concerns about the lack of normal investigative or criminal proceedings, and stayed silent for this new concept until a few months later, when he threatened to sue Webflicks if they continued the show.

_Damn him!_ Antoinette thought. This storyline worked so well against her involvement with _Criminal Cosby_ and _The Family Menudo_, there had to be something the writers could preserve even with this car theft taking away what was supposed to connect it all... maybe something else could be said about The Man's family they had overlooked earlier to make it all fit together again?

Dr. Burjest took some deep breaths, attempting to accept that The Man wasn't whom she was so desperate for him to be. After _Thought Patrol_ was cancelled, her new talent agent Art got Hoopla to pick her up for a docuseries called _Checkmate on Criminal Consciousness_. It was meant to introduce her real person to the media before the spin off of _Thought Patrol_ could be produced - they just had to prove something about The Man that was unprovable, provided you had access to the FBI's car theft database.

Was she set up? Did they do this on purpose?

She wondered if she was feeling all right - age creeps up on you, even in your eighties. In the 1980s, when she was in her forties and part of the FBI's BSU, she got used to looking monsters of all kinds in the eyes. She held close to her heart the quote about not letting yourself become a monster should you fight them. As Dr. Burjest stared at her reflection, she began to wonder if something else had gotten to her.

Had she been staring into another abyss without knowing?

...

"Fuck y'all! You dumb motherfuckers think you can drink from MY cup? Y'all can't drink from my cup! UH uh!" The Man shouted on the screens which the Reality TV company, Polonium, watched him 24/7 from the McMansions across the street. He was holding a coffee cup with a joke about `The Other Party`'s leader which could be read anyway one wanted to, politically speaking.

The Man had read _1984_ recently, commenting on it aloud as he read to the cameras and microphones which were recording him without his consent. As a result, much of Hollywood was abuzz anew about the book, in fact many had been during the entire filming of the show, which was filming without his written consent, but continued on, as did the surveillance state in _1984_, just at a wider scale.

Seeing as such, Raquel O'Brien gave Adria Fortley a recommendation to keep the filming crew focused - lean into it, the whole "Orwellian" thing. One idea that came from that was for the film crew to commit to a daily "15 minutes of hate." They usually waited for The Man to go on some kind of rant to emphasize the idea that it wasn't worth listening to him - they were here to judge, not to comprehend.

"Boo! We don't care if you could prove your innocence, we still hate you!"

"You corny!"

"You're not even that smart! It's not impressive you _just_ read _1984_, I read that in high school!"

"I read it in college!"

"Man, look at you, it's 2 PM and you haven't even showered!"

"Your breath stinks and we can feel it through the cameras!"

"You middle name must be 'he ain't shit'!"

"Well, he definitely ain't The Uno!"

"Man, if I could throw a stone at your dumb ass I would!"

"Me too!"

"I'm actually glad we get to make a show defaming you, and all without your consent! That's how much I hate you!"

"We're all gonna be more famous than you after this! Fuck _The Mimus_!"

"You corny!"

The Man, not able to hear or see any of this, maintained an attitude that seemed to counter that, at least for this particular "15 minutes of hate" session. He took a second for a sip of coffee, then continued with a bit more calm.

"Well, while I know you can't drink from my cup - someday you might have to" said The Man, taking another sip, then coming around to another subject.

"Hey, you know who I think has gotten really far away from perhaps once being a bit of a narcissist, you know, in that colloquial way at least? John Mayer - don'tcha think? But he's been famous for like, ever."

...

Monsters were on Dr. Burjest's mind as she looked into the mirror. She didn't know what Dugin meant a year ago, but yes, the situation was a very political one. That was the point, as Raquel told her, of the legal discipline hearing in NY that was to take place soon, in July. Disciplinary complaints and hearings against lawyers were separate from the criminal justice system, as they were conducted by the NY State Bar.

The thinking was that if an argument can be made with some sort of process involving a bunch of lawyers, it would more than likely be deferred to by an actual court, civil or criminal, similar to arbitration rulings. The legal discipline approach allowed the "unethical" medical research to continue, preventing The Man from making legal claims himself before `the Inner Party` could make an argument it would prefer, without The Man getting in the way by being able to represent himself. The discipline complaint framed the medical malpractice as a misdiagnosis, meaning it would not be considered criminal if the argument was later used in actual court, meaning, among other things, that Dr. Burjest's involvement would not be looked into outside of her expertise.

To avoid that, they either had to prove the misdiagnosis, which Dr. Burjest would not prefer, or to prove the original diagnosis that The Man was schizophrenic. However, she alongside former APA chief Dr. Jeffries hadn't been able to do that convincingly for over two years now. And that was before the car theft got in the way.

It wouldn't be easy with this new information, but it was still most important for the sake of Dr. Burjest's new found position within `The Inner Party` that there would be no criminal investigation. Seeing as such, she'd accept an outcome of a misdiagnosis if it came to that.

Suddenly in the mirror her face started to change, first melting like a Salvador Dalí painting, then taking some new shape. She first saw a younger version of herself, then she saw Anna Torv playing a younger version of herself, and perhaps it was age creeping up to her, but Dr. Burjest could swear she heard the image say something, though their lips weren't moving.

_The Menudos weren't innocent but they were traumatized, so you spoke for the defense... The Man is innocent, and his trauma may not be useful to you..._

Antoinette could swear the image was going to say something more, in fact, she could swear the image _had_ said something more, but it eluded her, the way a dream does as you wake up and move throughout the day.

And just like that, Dr. Burjest only saw herself in the mirror.

Dr. Burjest turned her thoughts away from any monsters inside and reminded herself of the last line in the email - she was still bound for the Tribeca Film Festival in a little over a week to give interviews on her new docuseries for Hoopla, _Checkmate on Criminal Consciousness._ That made her feel better, for the moment, and she returned to passing some time by peeling her orange.
