## Art Auerbach

Art is the "unhappy Hamlet" in the dueling Hamlet equation, where The Man is the "happy Hamlet." The dichotomy here is The Man has embraced things like therapy and personal growth, where Art has avoided it.

Art is at first comic relief, then as the novel continues, is one of the victims - Hollywood types have been for decades bullying him into not being able to grow out of his faults. Many throughout the novel feel this whole thing is Art's doing, when he is really trying to protect his Husband and a bunch of `Inner Party` types from being (correctly) diagnosed. In many ways, he is a tragic hero, and is The Man's counterpart.

### Writing King Auerbach

King Auerbach is Art's dead father's ghost, and the buried or ignored voice of Art's conscious.

Writing him will be a bit of a gag I'll at least give away in my notes. King Auerbach should respond to Art's thoughts, they should never clearly have any direct dialogue, though Art will respond in ways. If Art says something out loud, it should be to someone else or to himself perhaps. Art can also think thoughts in response either via _italics_ or via the narrator as well.

This is so it can never be truly read or confirmed that Art is indeed halucinating and the writer isn't playing some trick on you. Or, to suggest our conscious, when not listened to, forms some kind of ghost we go in and out of ignoring. I guess it's a reader's choice, if they pick up on it.
