## Raquel O'Brien

Raquel O'Brien starts the novel in her late 40s, fearing her 50s, which she enters as the novel continues. She is the lead anchor for PNN, the Pequod News Network, and has been for nearly 20 years. A female, gay, and gen X icon, Raquel has always been a perfectionist, whose accompanying feelings of inadequacy is the root of her narcissism[^1]. She is also a closeted bi polar, aware of her condition but fearing how a diagnosis could be used against her in the ultra competitive world of cable news. To compensate, she reads medical journals and attempts to treat her bi polar symptoms with self care. Her perfectionism leads her to constantly battle feelings of inadequacy, which she reacts to in fluctuations depending on whether she is in a manic or depressive cycle, and socially she controls via narcissistic positioning of herself as dominant over her peers, usually via her high level of intelligence and quick wit, the latter of which is heightened during manic cycles.

### Raquel's Narcissistic Jealousy cycles w/ The Man

She starts the novel wanting to frame the man as per her first reality TV show, an entry into her dream career of being a creative type. However, this backfires, inflaming her feelings of inadequacy (the root of her perfectionism), as she wanted to frame the man for being a "hacker" to cover up for the time in 2017 she proclaimed a foreign national threat via hacking that never surfaced in reality.

Her jealousy of the man begins in late 2022, as he, however unknowingly to him, ingratiates himself with his knowledge of references, particularly _The Man Who Was Thursday_, and it's influence over art in various ways amongst a crowd to which Raquel aspires to make her peers - Hollywood writers, directors, and actors. The man's understanding of the novel infuriates Raquel on top of making her jealous of how it woos a crowd she wishes to woo, the fury coming from Raquel's being in a situation where she needs The Man to either be akin to the novel's surrealistic "dynamiters," but in reality and not surreality, freeing her from being amongst the delusional dreamers in the novel - or at least, akin to the novel's main character, Thursday, who is a guy who _definitely_ wouldn't get the novel was about him overreacting to being rejected by a girl in the beginning.

When further in 2022, the Man also holds the unsubstantiated claims of foreign hacking against Raquel, without knowing she was listening or involved in his life in anyway, this tilts Raquel into a co-morbidity of paranoid schizophrenia, as she begins to wonder if the Man knows she's behind not only the Reality TV show, but her initial botched attempt to frame him with it back in May of 2022. In reality, he's just voicing one of the reasons he stopped trusting PNN, in a thwarted hope that he could get PNN to cancel the show if they heard of their journalistic integrity being attacked.

Raquel keeps all of the paranoia to herself, however, in a fear of being diagnosed.

The continued failure of her attempt to use the Man for her own ascent to a different type of fame, against her initial reasoning of wanting to cover up a previous flaw (unsubstantiated claims of foreign hacking) which she perceives as greater than it is due to her perfectionism, against the push / pull of unmanaged mania and depressive cycles that have become inflamed due to the freedom of her new contract with PNN, (4 days off, with pay, to which Raquel fails to return an investment, initiating further feelings of inadequacy) all incite Raquel into NPD.

Finally, Raquel is constantly pushed into jealousy cycles due to the musicians who are making albums around a number game assigned by "birth cards" - her number would be 7, as she is a 7 of spades, but the 7th track always goes to a potential romantic interest of the Man's, someone Raquel also sees as not a human but a stepping stone to her greater success, thus further forcing Raquel to feel inadequate in a way that inflames her narcissism, considering she is on a tier of fame Raquel presupposes to be on the level of the musicians', yet, she is not a center of focus in the album game. Much of her identity which separates her as a newscaster is for her taste in music, curated for maximum cool - that she can't be included in the game hurts her in many ways, narcissisticly and situationally speaking. She seeks to sabotage The Man's chances with said 7 as a form of narcissistic retaliation against The Man, which is truly just her acting against the day she was born - April Fools.

These jealousy cycles inspire Raquel's descent to losing any empathy for those she perceives as beneath her, while her want to use those beneath her to be with those she perceives as above her in Hollywood and politics pushes Raquel into a cycle of "kissing up and kicking down"[^2], later motivating her to use her connections to force The Man into a legal limbo of `the Inner Party` and her own control.

### Raquel's relationship to O'Brien in _1984_

At some point she's gonna have to give a speech about how it's good, actually, that `the Inner Party` is more focused on power than political progress, even at the expense of both. This speech is meant to be given possibly to some character Raquel has led to believe her efforts are for the best of `the Inner Party`'s political goals, which said character calls out is clearly not the case after the massive election loss. It's intended to mirror the O'Brien speech in 1984, but in the over emphasized ironic detachment Generation X is known for, and will likely reference it directly as positive, which will be delivered with ironic humour by Raquel to cover up from the obvious fascism it implies.

### Raquel's other issues with the man

She hates how stereotypically "broish" he is, yet appreciated for the minor ways in which isn't, further emphasizing her inability to view the Man as a human being instead of a set of stereotypes, as the latter often works positively for her various iconic statuses.

She hates much of The Man's taste in music, particularly his love for artists that couldn't be considered as "underground" during her own youth yet are perceived to be by many in the present day as "niche" (reinforcing her notion that she is superior in knowledge to others via her taste), and considers it without knowing an attack on her identity as a Gen X icon. 

### Voicing Raquel

Narration in chapters where Raquel is in a manic cycle should be voiced somewhat Dickensian - sentences with clever additions and offshoots via dash / parenthesis breaks, strike through maybe, etc - to elaborate how witty Raquel is when manic, capable of bouncing between multiple tangents while still landing on a point in the end. When she is in a depressive cycle - if I'm feeling up to it - she should represent a Wharton-esque Lily Barth on tilt, as near the end of _House of Mirth_, where the same tangents that were once so delightful and funny focus on her self esteem issues.

### Raquel's Conscious as a Surreality

All the narcissistic characters should have surreal breaks where what would be their conscious speaks to them. This is to emphasize that narcissists struggle with empathy and thus don't hear what most consider to be "the voice of their conscious," which would prevent them from acting as awfully as they do towards others. The surreal aspect is to show that it's surreal to expect narcissists to even struggle with a conscious, hopefully.
